<h3>Population-Level Modeling of Long COVID Burden</h3>

<p class="research-tags">
  Long COVID · Epidemiological Modeling · State-Transition Modeling · Population Health · Wastewater Surveillance
</p>

<p><strong>Ongoing Research · 2025–Present</strong></p>

<p>
  Long COVID has created a substantial and persistent public health burden, but estimating its population-level impact remains challenging because reported COVID-19 cases do not fully capture the true scale of acute infections.
</p>

<p>
  My current research develops a <strong>population-level modeling framework for Long COVID</strong> that connects acute COVID-19 infection dynamics with post-acute disease progression and longer-term symptom burden.
</p>

<p>
  A major focus of this work is integrating <strong>wastewater-informed estimates of SARS-CoV-2 transmission</strong> with a state-transition framework for Long COVID. By combining population-level infection estimates with heterogeneous post-acute symptom trajectories, the model is designed to better characterize how Long COVID burden accumulates and evolves over time.
</p>

<p>
  The framework incorporates longitudinal symptom patterns informed by clinical evidence and follows individuals through different trajectories of persistent symptoms, improvement, recovery, or continued disease burden. These individual-level trajectories are then aggregated to estimate population-level outcomes such as Long COVID prevalence and active disease burden over calendar time.
</p>

<p>
  Ultimately, this work aims to provide a more comprehensive understanding of the long-term consequences of COVID-19 and to support future evaluation of healthcare needs and public health interventions.
</p>

<h3>Drinking-Behavior-Driven Microsimulation for Alcohol-Related Disease Progression</h3>

<p class="research-tags">
  Microsimulation · Alcohol Use · Disease Progression · Population Health · Intervention Evaluation
</p>

<p><strong>Ongoing Research Program · 2024–Present</strong></p>

<p>
  This is an ongoing research program centered on an individual-level microsimulation framework that links <strong>drinking behavior, chronic disease progression, mortality, and healthcare interventions</strong>.
</p>

<p>
  Rather than treating alcohol-related diseases as isolated outcomes, the framework follows individuals across heterogeneous drinking-risk states and disease trajectories over time. It is designed to study how changes in alcohol consumption influence long-term health outcomes across U.S. birth cohorts and to evaluate interventions aimed at reducing alcohol-related disease burden.
</p>

<p>
  The framework has continued to evolve as new disease pathways, mortality risks, and intervention strategies are incorporated.
</p>

    <p>

      The model is used to examine how heterogeneous drinking patterns and disease trajectories contribute to mortality over time and across different U.S. birth cohorts.

<h3>Intra-Subject Respiratory Motion Modeling Using a Diffeomorphic Approach</h3>

<p class="research-tags">
  Medical Imaging · 4D CT · Image Registration · Diffeomorphic Modeling · Optimization
</p>

<p>
  Respiratory motion creates an important challenge for image-guided lung interventions. A conventional static CT scan captures the anatomy at only one moment in the respiratory cycle, while lung tissue, vessels, and potential biopsy targets continuously move as a patient breathes.
</p>

<p>
  In this project, we developed a computational framework for modeling <strong>patient-specific respiratory motion from 4D CT images</strong>. The goal was to reconstruct the dynamic motion of lung regions across respiratory phases and provide more informative motion estimates for applications such as image-guided lung biopsy.
</p>

<p>The modeling pipeline included:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Extracting anatomical information from <strong>4D CT images</strong> across different respiratory phases</li>
  <li>Constructing point-cloud representations of lung structures and identifying anatomical landmarks for motion correspondence</li>
  <li>Using <strong>affine transformations</strong> to characterize global respiratory motion between image phases</li>
  <li>Fitting correspondence models using both <strong>linear and nonlinear least-squares optimization</strong></li>
  <li>Representing local tissue deformation using <strong>deformable vector fields (DVFs)</strong></li>
  <li>Applying a <strong>diffeomorphic modeling framework</strong> to preserve smooth and anatomically meaningful transformations</li>
  <li>Comparing the derived motion fields with reference vector fields to evaluate motion-model accuracy</li>
</ul>

<p>
  A key part of the project was developing and validating DVFs that describe how individual regions of the lung move during respiration. These motion fields provide a continuous representation of tissue displacement and can potentially help estimate the location of anatomical targets when direct imaging information is limited.
</p>

<p>
  The project began as my undergraduate senior project at <strong>Sichuan University–Pittsburgh Institute</strong> and later developed into a peer-reviewed conference publication.
</p>

<h3>Airway Tree Segmentation Using U²-Net</h3>

<p class="research-tags">
  Deep Learning · Medical Imaging · CT · U²-Net · PyTorch
</p>

<p>
  This project focused on automatic <strong>airway tree segmentation from chest CT scans</strong>, with the goal of accurately extracting both the main trachea and smaller peripheral bronchi from three-dimensional medical images.
</p>

<p>
  We developed a deep-learning segmentation pipeline based on <strong>U²-Net</strong>, a nested U-shaped convolutional neural network architecture. The model was trained using multi-site CT scans from the <strong>ATM'22 Airway Tree Modeling Challenge</strong>, which provided 299 annotated CT scans for training and 50 additional scans for validation.
</p>

<p>
  A major challenge in airway segmentation is the strong <strong>class imbalance</strong> between airway and non-airway voxels, since airway structures occupy only a small portion of a chest CT scan. To address this, we trained the U²-Net using the <strong>Dice loss function</strong> rather than conventional binary cross-entropy, allowing the model to place greater emphasis on accurately identifying small airway structures.
</p>

<p>The workflow included:</p>

<ul>
  <li>CT intensity normalization and preprocessing to improve training efficiency and feature extraction</li>
  <li>A two-level nested <strong>U²-Net architecture</strong> with Residual U-blocks (RSUs) for multi-scale feature learning</li>
  <li>Multiple side-output saliency probability maps combined into a final airway probability map</li>
  <li>Dice-loss-based end-to-end optimization to address airway/non-airway class imbalance</li>
  <li>Thresholding and connected-region post-processing to remove spurious non-airway regions and refine the final airway tree</li>
</ul>

<p>
  The resulting model demonstrated good segmentation of the <strong>trachea and major bronchial branches</strong>, with generally strong connectivity and anatomical accuracy on the validation CT scans, while also highlighting the greater difficulty of preserving connectivity in smaller peripheral bronchi.
</p>

<h3>Flu Hospitalization Forecasting</h3>

<p class="research-tags">
  Epidemiological Modeling · Forecasting · SIRS · Extended Kalman Filter · Time Series
</p>

<p>
  This project focused on probabilistic forecasting of <strong>seasonal influenza hospitalizations</strong> by combining epidemiological modeling with real-time data sources, including Google search trends.
</p>

<p>
  I worked with a compartmental <strong>SIRS model</strong> together with an <strong>Extended Kalman Filter (EKF)</strong> to improve state estimation and dynamically update hospitalization forecasts as new observations became available.
</p>

<p>The broader forecasting framework explored multiple modeling approaches, including:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Naive forecasting strategies</li>
  <li>Compartmental epidemiological models</li>
  <li>Statistical time-series methods</li>
  <li>Machine learning approaches</li>
  <li>Temporal deep-learning models</li>
  <li>Multivariable temporal forecasting models</li>
</ul>

<p>
  My work focused particularly on integrating the SIRS model with the EKF for real-time data assimilation, allowing the latent epidemic states to be continuously updated while accounting for uncertainty and noisy observations.
</p>

<p>
  The project also involved processing and correcting CDC flu hospitalization data to support more reliable forecasting and evaluation.
</p>
